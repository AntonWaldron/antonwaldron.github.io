Multilevel Monte Carlo Methods for Option Pricing

An investigation of multilevel Monte Carlo methods as a more computationally efficient approach to pricing European, American, Asian, lookback and digital options.

About the project

In applications, standard Monte Carlo is sometimes referred to as brute-force Monte Carlo, reflecting the substantial computational cost of obtaining accurate estimates. This is particularly relevant in option pricing, where a large number of sample paths may be required. Multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) addresses this problem by repeatedly applying the identity \(\mathbb{E}[X] = \mathbb{E}[Y] + \mathbb{E}[X-Y]\). This produces a telescoping decomposition in which estimates calculated on inexpensive, coarse levels are successively corrected using the differences between adjacent levels. An accessible illustration is provided in the poster, where MLMC is used to estimate the constant \(\pi\).

I implemented MLMC in MATLAB and investigated how the method could be adapted to different option types and payoff structures. Milstein discretisation and Brownian bridges were applied to lookback options, producing an efficiency improvement of up to 136 times over standard Monte Carlo at low RMSE targets. I also combined MLMC with Least Squares Monte Carlo for American options, achieving an improvement of up to approximately two times faster convergence. The project exposed me to Monte Carlo methods for option pricing and allowed me to develop an understanding of their adaptability across different settings, notably for American options and under both the Black–Scholes and Heston models.

Time Series Forecast for Darts Interest

Inspired by Luke Littler's rise to fame, our group decided to choose 'Interest', defined by Google searches, as our Time Series for this project. Upon analysis of the series, it is clear that it is not stationary and a Box-Cox transform is applied to stabilise the variance. However, this results in the series exhibiting a negative trend and first order differencing is required, still the series exhibits a seasonal trend and thus a seasonal differencing is required. The result is a stationary series and upon analysis of the ACF and PACF plots we deduce that an \( \operatorname{ARIMA}(0,1,1)\times(0,1,1)_{12} \) model is optimal. Predicting Bike Rentals with Linear Regression

This was a class project from the Advanced Data Analysis module. In the first section, we were each given a specific set of variables to analyse. Mine were the Season, Hour, Rain and Tempurature, and I visually assessed their relationship with the number of bike rentals. In the next section, we were given the freedom to fit our own linear regression model. Through visual analysis and exploration of the variable space, I identified that there was a statistically significant interaction between the continuous variable humidity and the indicator variable is_weekend;. This makes sense intuitively, as during the week many people likely rent a bike for essential transport, whereas weekend rentals are likely more discretionary and therefore more sensitive to weather conditions. The final model included humidity, time of day, the season, whether it was raining, and the interaction term between humidity and the weekend status. Analysis of the residuals showed that they were approximately normally distributed.

Is the deliberate practice method of "Aim Training" associated with a higher rank in the competitive videogame Valorant?

This was a very fun project to do; at the time I was interested in both competitive gaming and the concept of deliberate practice, having recently read Geoff Calvin's book 'Talent is Overrated'. Therefore I was genuinely interested in the question of whether or not Aim Training (the practice of using external software to get better at "aiming" i.e. moving your mouse to click on objects on the screen), a deliberate practice technique, was predictive of rank. To answer this question I collected data using the following survey: 1. What is your current Valorant rank? 2. How many hours have you played in aim training software? 3. A Likert scale of experience prior to playing Valorant. 4. How many hours have you played in Valorant? Using a Chi-Square test I was able to determine that there was a statistically significant difference between the ranks of "Aim Trainers" (defined as having played more than 50 hours in aim training software) and non-"Aim Trainers". The goal was to perform an ANOVA but due to a violation of the assumption of normality, a Kruskal-Wallis test was used instead which indicated a statistically significant difference in median aim training times across ranks in Valorant, however subsequent Mann-Whitney U Test's did not reveal a statistically significant difference between specific ranks.
